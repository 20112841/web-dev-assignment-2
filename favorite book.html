My favourite book

My favorite book is No longer human - Osamu Dazai
Mine has been a life of much shame.
I can't even guess myself what it must be to live the life of a human being. Portraying himself as a failure, the protagonist of Osamu Dazai's No Longer Human narrates a seemingly normal life even while he feels himself incapable of understanding human beings. His attempts to reconcile himself to the world around him begin in early childhood, continue through high school, where he becomes a "clown" to mask his alienation, and eventually lead to a failed suicide attempt as an adult. Without sentimentality, he records the casual cruelties of life and its fleeting moments of human connection and tenderness. Still one of the ten bestselling books in Japan, No Longer Human is an important and unforgettable modern classic:
"The struggle of the individual to fit into a normalizing society remains just as relevant today as it was at the time of writing."
- The Japan Times.
I aboslutely love this book. To a certain extent, I feel like I can relate to his feelings of isolation and his inability to fit in to society. I'm a huge introvert and would rather stay inside rather than going on a night out for example. It takes a lot of energy out of me to go to college every day. I usually feel drained after a week. Some people call this a "social battery". The thought of having to be around many people for long periods of time just evokes a sense of dread within me that I cant explain, Which is a big reason on why I chose to study computer science, as I'd like to eventually work remotely. However, that's the extent to which i relate to the main character in this book, otherwise, the character is a deplorable human with little to no sympathy for others, going as far as manipulating the women in his life and even not intervening in the sexual assault of his wife. This book is basically an autobriography of the authors life, in other words, a suicide note. The author also took his life in the same way that the character in this book. This book is almost a one to one account of the authors life, with some scenes exagerated. It was the first piece of Japanese literature I have read and perhaps the first piece of literature in general I've read, and ignited that passion for reading for me, which is why I will always love this book dearly.
